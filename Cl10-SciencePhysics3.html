Refraction & Dispersion by a Prism

Dispersion of White Light

White light consists of 7 colors. Different colors of light bend through different angles with respect to the incident ray. Red bends the least, Violet bends the most.

🌈

Rainbow Formation

A rainbow is a natural spectrum appearing in the sky after a rain shower. It is caused by dispersion of sunlight by tiny water droplets, present in the atmosphere.

1

Refraction & Dispersion: Sunlight enters a water droplet, refracting and splitting into constituent colors.

2

Total Internal Reflection: The light hits the back of the droplet and reflects entirely internally.

3

Final Refraction: The light refracts again as it exits the droplet, creating the rainbow we see.

Crucial Condition: You can only see a rainbow if the Sun is behind you.

4. Atmospheric Refraction

The earth's atmosphere is not uniform. The optical density increases as we go lower (hotter air is lighter, cooler air is denser). When starlight enters the atmosphere, it undergoes continuous refraction, bending towards the normal.

⭐ Why do Stars Twinkle?

The physical conditions of the earth's atmosphere are not stationary. The apparent position of the star fluctuates, and the amount of starlight entering the eye flickers (sometimes brighter, sometimes fainter). Planets are closer and act as extended sources, thus their total variation averages out to zero (no twinkling).

🌅 Advanced Sunrise & Delayed Sunset

The Sun is visible to us about 2 minutes before actual sunrise, and about 2 minutes after actual sunset because of atmospheric refraction. We see the apparent image of the sun slightly above the horizon.

5. Scattering of Light

The interplay of light with objects around us gives rise to spectacular phenomena. This is based on the Tyndall Effect and Rayleigh scattering.

☀️

Mid-Day

Sun is overhead. Light travels less distance through the atmosphere. Only a little blue and violet light is scattered. Sun appears white.

The Tyndall Effect

The earth's atmosphere is a heterogeneous mixture of minute particles (smoke, water droplets, dust, air molecules). When a beam of light strikes such fine particles, the path of the beam becomes visible.

Why is the clear sky blue?

Molecules of air (N₂, O₂) have sizes smaller than the wavelength of visible light. They are more effective in scattering light of shorter wavelengths at the blue end. The scattered blue light enters our eyes.

Color of Sun at Sunrise/Sunset

Light from the sun near the horizon passes through thicker layers of air and larger distance. Most of the blue light and shorter wavelengths are scattered away by the particles. Therefore, the light that reaches our eyes is of longer wavelengths (red). This gives rise to the reddish appearance.

Danger Signals: "Danger" signal lights are red in colour. Do you know why? Red is least scattered by fog or smoke. Therefore, it can be seen in the same colour at a distance.
