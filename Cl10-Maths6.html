Triangles

Two triangles with the same shape but different sizes are called similar. This chapter shows how a single parallel line, or two matching angles, is enough to prove it — and why that lets you measure a tower from its shadow.

6.2 · Similar figures

02Same shape, scaled

All circles are similar. All squares are similar. All equilateral triangles are similar. But not all rectangles are, and not all triangles are — the shape itself must match.

Always

Circles, squares, equilateral triangles

Any two of these have the same shape, whatever their size.

Sometimes

Rectangles, isosceles triangles

A long thin rectangle is not similar to a square-ish one, even though both are rectangles.

Rule

Two conditions

Corresponding angles equal, and corresponding sides in the same ratio. For triangles, the first condition alone is enough.

6.3 · The Basic Proportionality Theorem

03One parallel line does the work

Draw a triangle, then draw a line inside it parallel to the base. It cuts the two other sides in exactly the same ratio. This is Thales' theorem, and it is the engine behind most proofs in the chapter.

If DE ∥ BC, then ADDB = AEEC

The converse also holds: if a line divides two sides in the same ratio, it must be parallel to the third side.

Proof sketch

Join BE and CD. Triangles BDE and CDE share the same base DE and the same height (because DE is parallel to BC, so both sit between the same parallels). Equal areas.

Triangles ADE and BDE share a height from E, so their areas are in the ratio of the bases AD : DB. Similarly for ADE and CDE with bases AE : EC. Equating the two gives the theorem.

The consequence you actually use

Because the ratios match, you can set up a proportion and solve for the missing length:

AD/DB = AE/EC → AD = (AE × DB)/EC

Cross-multiplication is all the algebra this theorem needs.

6.4 · The three tests

04AA, SSS, SAS

You do not have to check all six things (three angles, three sides). Any one of these three combinations is enough.

AA (or AAA)

Two angles of one triangle equal two angles of the other. The third pair then follows automatically.

SSS

All three pairs of corresponding sides are in the same ratio.

SAS

Two pairs of sides in the same ratio, and the angle between them equal.

Worked example

05The tower and its shadow

A vertical pole 6 m tall casts a shadow 4 m long. At the same moment a tower casts a shadow 28 m long. How tall is the tower?

Step-through · shadow problem
Sunlight makes the same angle with everything at the same moment.
The triangles. The pole and its shadow form a right triangle; the tower and its shadow form another.
Why similar. Both have a right angle at the base, and the sun's rays make equal angles with the ground. By AA, the triangles are similar.
Write the ratio. height/shadow is the same for both: 6/4 = h/28.
Solve. h = 6 × 28/4 = 42 m.
Why this trick works in real life At any given moment the sun's rays are parallel, so every vertical object and its shadow form triangles with the same angles. That is how heights of buildings were measured long before lasers.

Before the exam

07Four traps

01

Name the vertices in order

ΔABC ~ ΔPQR means A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R. Writing the letters in the wrong order makes every ratio wrong.

02

Similar ≠ congruent

Congruent triangles are similar with ratio 1. Similar ones usually differ in size.

03

SSA is not a test

Two sides and a non-included angle do not guarantee similarity. Use SAS with the included angle.

04

State the criterion

“By AA” or “by SAS” earns the method mark. Jumping straight to the ratio can lose it.

Chapter summary

13Five lines to revise

  • Similar: equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
  • BPT: a line parallel to one side divides the other two in the same ratio.
  • Converse: equal ratios imply the line is parallel.
  • Criteria: AA, SSS, SAS.
  • Name triangles with matching vertices, or every ratio will be wrong.
