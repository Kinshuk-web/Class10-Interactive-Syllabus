1. Nervous System: Structure of a Neuron & Synapse

The nervous tissue is made up of an organized network of nerve cells or neurons. They conduct information via electrical and chemical impulses across synaptic gaps.

Click on any labeled structure in the diagram to inspect its role.

Dendrite

Receptor End

Dendrites are branch-like projections at the receiving end of a neuron. They acquire information/stimuli from sensory receptors or adjacent neurons and convert it into an electrical impulse.

Board Exam Tip: Information always travels in a unidirectional manner: Dendrite $\rightarrow$ Cell Body $\rightarrow$ Axon $\rightarrow$ Axonal End $\rightarrow$ Synapse.

2. Involuntary Response: Reflex Arc

A reflex action is an extremely quick, automatic, and sudden involuntary response to a stimulus (e.g. touching a boiling hot flame) without conscious thinking by the brain.

Reflex Arc Step-by-Step Simulator

Click "Trigger Stimulus" to observe how the impulse travels through the spinal cord before reaching the brain.

1
Receptors

Heat/Pain receptors in the skin detect the dangerous stimulus.

2
Sensory Neuron

Carries the electrical signal from skin to the Spinal Cord.

3
Spinal Cord (Relay)

Relay neuron processes signal immediately in spinal cord.

4
Motor Neuron

Transmits the response signal from spinal cord to arm muscles.

5
Effector (Muscle)

Muscle contracts instantly, withdrawing the hand from danger.

Why are Reflex Arcs formed in the Spinal Cord instead of the Brain?
Thinking by the complex neural network of the brain takes time. Since detecting heat and thinking about moving the hand could cause severe burns, reflex arcs evolved in animals as quick, efficient response mechanisms formed directly at the spinal cord!

3. Human Brain & Central Nervous System

The brain is the main coordinating center of the human body. Protected inside the fluid-filled skull (cranium), it has three major regions: Forebrain, Midbrain, and Hindbrain.

Forebrain (Cerebrum)

Main Thinking Part

The Forebrain is the most complex and specialized part of the brain. It consists of the Cerebrum. It receives sensory impulses from visual, auditory, and olfactory receptors, and controls all voluntary actions like thinking, memory, learning, and reasoning.

Center for intelligence, memory, hearing, smell, and sight.

4. Coordination in Plants

Unlike animals, plants have neither a nervous system nor muscles! They show two distinct types of movement: Tropic movements (directional, growth-dependent) and Nastic movements (immediate, growth-independent).

Phototropism & Auxin Bending

Growth Dependent

When growing plants detect light, a hormone called Auxin synthesized at the shoot tip helps the cells grow longer. Auxin diffuses towards the shaded side of the shoot, stimulating cell elongation there and causing the stem to bend towards light.

Auxin evenly distributed at tip

Nastic Movement (*Mimosa pudica*)

Growth Independent

When we touch the leaves of 'Touch-me-not' (*Chhui-mui*), they fold up and droop immediately. Plants use electrical-chemical means to convey information and change the amount of water (turgor pressure) in their cells, leading to swelling or shrinking!

🌿

Click the leaf above to touch it!

Geotropism: Growth response to gravity (roots grow down $+$, shoots grow up $-$).
Hydrotropism: Roots bending towards water sources.
Chemotropism: Growth of pollen tubes towards ovules during fertilization.
Thigmotropism: Tendrils clinging around a support for climb.

NCERT Plant Hormones (Phytohormones) Matrix

Hormone Category Primary Function NCERT Board Key Point
Auxin Promoter Promotes cell elongation at shoot tips. Helps in phototropism. Concentrates on shaded side of shoot.
Gibberellins Promoter Promotes growth of stem, internode elongation, and seed germination. Works in tandem with Auxin for stem height.
Cytokinins Promoter Promotes rapid cell division. Present in high concentration in fruits and seeds.
Abscisic Acid (ABA) Inhibitor Inhibits growth, causes wilting of leaves, and induces seed dormancy. Known as the "Stress Hormone" in plants.

5. Hormones in Animals: Human Endocrine System

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted directly into the bloodstream by ductless endocrine glands to reach target organs.

Click any gland on the body diagram to inspect its hormones.

Thyroid Gland

Hormone: Thyroxine

Located attached to the windpipe in the neck. Thyroxine regulates carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism in the body to provide the best balance for growth.

Iodine Deficiency: Iodine is essential for thyroxine synthesis. Lack of iodine leads to Goitre (swollen neck).

Hormone Feedback Mechanism (Blood Sugar Control)

Hormones must be released in precise quantities. The timing and amount of hormone released are regulated by feedback mechanisms.

Trigger
High Blood Sugar

Glucose rises after eating meals.

Detection
Pancreas Cells

Detect elevated blood glucose levels.

Response
Insulin Secreted

Pancreas produces and secretes Insulin.

Result
Sugar Level Falls

Insulin secretion stops via negative feedback.

Conceptual

What is a synapse? How does a chemical signal transmit across it?

Model Answer

A synapse is a micro gap between the axon terminal of one neuron and the dendrite of the next. When an electrical impulse reaches the axonal end, it triggers the release of chemical neurotransmitters across the gap, generating a new electrical impulse in the next neuron.
