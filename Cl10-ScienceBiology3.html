NCERT Section 7.1

Do Organisms Create Exact Copies of Themselves?

Reproduction involves creating a DNA blueprint copy along with a cellular apparatus. Biochemical copying reactions are precise, but not 100% accurate!

DNA Copying & Biochemical Variation

Chromosomes in the nucleus contain DNA (Deoxyribo Nucleic Acid), which is the information source for making proteins. If information changes, different proteins are made, leading to altered body designs.

Cellular Apparatus Creation: DNA copying is always accompanied by the creation of an additional cellular apparatus so each daughter cell gets its own operational machinery.
Survival vs Drastic Errors: Drastic copying errors cause the newborn cell to die because it can't work with its apparatus. Minor variations survive, giving subtle differences!

Modes of Asexual Reproduction

Single organisms create new generations without gamete fusion. Explore each mode with interactive step-by-step visual models.

NCERT Fig 7.1

Binary Fission

Amoeba / Leishmania

Unicellular organisms divide into two equal halves. In Amoeba, division takes place in any plane. In Leishmania, binary fission occurs in a definite orientation relative to its whip-like flagellum.

NCERT Exam Key Point

Plasmodium (malarial parasite) divides simultaneously into many daughter cells by Multiple Fission inside a protective cyst.

What happens when the Egg is NOT Fertilized?

NCERT Section 7.3.3(c) • 28-Day Cycle

The unfertilized egg lives for about 1 day. The uterine lining thickens and becomes spongy every month to receive a zygote. If fertilization does not happen, this lining breaks down and exits through the vagina as blood & mucous (Menstruation, lasting 2 to 8 days).

NCERT Section 7.3.3(d)

Reproductive Health & Contraception

Preventing unwanted pregnancies and sexually transmitted diseases (STDs).

Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)

Sexually transmitted infections include Bacterial infections (Gonorrhoea, Syphilis) and Viral infections (Warts, HIV-AIDS). Condoms (barrier method) help prevent transmission of many infections!

1. Mechanical Barrier

Prevents sperm from physically meeting the egg.

  • Condoms (Penis / Vagina)
  • Protects against STDs!

2. Hormonal Pills

Changes hormonal balance so eggs are not released.

  • Oral Pills
  • Can cause side-effects

3. Intrauterine Devices

Placed inside the uterus to prevent pregnancy.

  • Loop & Copper-T
  • Uterine irritation risk

4. Surgical Methods

Blocks gamete transport permanently.

  • Vasectomy (Male vas deferens)
  • Tubectomy (Female fallopian tube)

Social Aspects & Sex Ratio

Prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law due to illegal female foeticides. Maintaining healthy female-male sex ratio is vital for society.

Activity 7.1

Yeast Sugar Fermentation Culture

Dissolve 10g sugar in 100mL water + pinch of yeast. Plug with cotton in warm place for 1-2 hours. Observe drop under microscope.

Observation: Yeast cells grow rapidly and put out small buds that separate into new individuals.
Activity 7.2

Bread Mould (Rhizopus) Culture

Wet a slice of bread and keep in cool, moist, dark place for a week. Observe white thread-like hyphae and black sporangia blobs.

Observation: Spore formation in Rhizopus via sporangia blobs atop non-reproductive hyphae threads.
Activity 7.3

Amoeba Binary Fission Slides

Observe permanent slide of normal Amoeba vs Amoeba undergoing binary fission under microscope.

Observation: Karyokinesis (nucleus division) followed by Cytokinesis (cytoplasm splitting) in any plane.
Activity 7.4

Spirogyra Pond Water Filaments

Collect dark green pond water with filaments. Mount on slide with glycerine drop and coverslip.

Observation: Filamentous Spirogyra breaks into smaller fragments upon maturation to form new individuals.
Activity 7.5 & 7.6

Potato Eyes & Money Plant Cuttings

Place potato pieces with vs without notch/bud on wet cotton. Cut money plant with vs without leaf node.

Observation: Only pieces containing buds/notches give rise to fresh green shoots and roots.
Activity 7.7

Bengal Gram (Chana) Germination

Soak gram seeds overnight, wrap in wet cloth for a day. Carefully cut open to identify cotyledons, plumule, radicle.

Observation: Cotyledons store food, Plumule grows into shoot, Radicle grows into root.

NCERT Page 114

What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?

NCERT Model Answer

DNA copying is essential because: 1. It transfers genetic blueprints for body design from parents to offspring. 2. It is accompanied by cellular apparatus creation so daughter cells can survive. 3. Subtle inaccuracies during DNA replication create variations essential for species survival and evolution.
