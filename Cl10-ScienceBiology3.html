Chapter Introduction

Why do organisms reproduce at all?

Reproduction is not necessary to maintain the life of an individual — unlike nutrition, respiration or excretion. In fact, creating new individuals costs a lot of energy! Yet organisms reproduce, and it is because of the large numbers of individuals in a species that we even notice them. Reproduction keeps the population of a species stable and sustains it in its ecological niche. If a niche changes drastically (temperature rise, water level change, meteorite hits), only populations with variations have a chance to survive.

NCERT Section 7.1

Do Organisms Create Exact Copies of Themselves?

Reproduction involves creating a DNA blueprint copy along with a cellular apparatus. Biochemical copying reactions are precise, but not 100% accurate!

DNA Copying & Biochemical Variation

Chromosomes in the nucleus contain DNA (Deoxyribo Nucleic Acid), which is the information source for making proteins. If information changes, different proteins are made, leading to altered body designs. A basic event in reproduction is the creation of a DNA copy.

Cellular Apparatus Creation: DNA copying is always accompanied by the creation of an additional cellular apparatus so each daughter cell gets its own operational machinery — effectively, a cell divides to give rise to two cells.
Survival vs Drastic Errors: Drastic copying errors cause the newborn cell to die because it can't work with its apparatus. Minor variations survive, giving subtle differences — this inbuilt tendency for variation is the basis of evolution.

Why the Sexual Mode of Reproduction?

Bulls alone cannot produce calves, nor can hens alone produce chicks. Two individuals are needed — here's the elegant DNA logic behind it.

Problem 1: Variation is slow

DNA-copying is precise, so making variants by copying errors alone is very slow. If copying were less accurate, most copies wouldn't work with the cellular apparatus and would die!

Solution: Combine two individuals

Two different individuals carry different accumulated variations (all harmless, since they live in working individuals). Combining them creates novel combinations — a much faster way to generate variation. That is the sexual mode!

Problem 2: DNA would double!

If each generation combined DNA from two parents, DNA content would keep doubling. Solution: special germ-cell lineages divide by meiosis to hold only half the chromosomes & DNA. Fusion of two germ-cells re-establishes the full number in the zygote.

Germ-cells specialise as bodies get complex

The zygote needs energy stores to grow into an organism with specialised tissues. So in complex organisms, germ-cells specialise:

Male gamete

Small & motile — mainly genetic material with a long tail that helps it move towards the female germ-cell (e.g., the human sperm).

Female gamete

Large & food-storing — contains the stored food needed by the future embryo (e.g., the egg cell inside the ovule).

This need to create two gamete types gives rise to differences in male & female reproductive organs — and sometimes whole bodies.

Sperm structure

The sperm is a tiny body consisting of mainly genetic material and a long tail that helps it move towards the female germ-cell.

Unisexual vs Bisexual Flowers:

Papaya & Watermelon are unisexual (contain either stamen or pistil). Hibiscus & Mustard are bisexual (contain both).

Placenta (NCERT High Yield):

A disc embedded in the uterine wall containing villi on the embryo side and blood spaces on mother side. Provides large surface area for glucose/O₂ supply and waste removal. Development takes about nine months; the child is born by rhythmic contractions of the uterine muscles.

What happens when the Egg is NOT Fertilized?

NCERT Section 7.3.3(c) • 28-Day Cycle

The unfertilized egg lives for about 1 day. The uterine lining thickens and becomes spongy every month to receive a zygote. If fertilization does not happen, this lining breaks down and exits through the vagina as blood & mucous (Menstruation, lasting 2 to 8 days). Since the ovary releases one egg every month, the uterus also prepares itself every month — this cycle repeats roughly every month.

Puberty — The Body Signals Sexual Maturation

As the rate of general body growth slows down, reproductive tissues begin to mature. This period during adolescence is called puberty.

Common to Boys & Girls

  • Thick hair grows in new parts — armpits and the genital area (which may darken)
  • Thinner hair appears on legs, arms and face
  • Skin frequently becomes oily; pimples may develop
  • New self-awareness of one's own and others' bodies

In Girls

  • Breast size begins to increase
  • Skin of the nipples at the tips of the breasts darkens
  • Menstruation begins at around this time

In Boys

  • New thick hair growth on the face
  • Voice begins to crack
  • Occasional enlargement & erection of the penis (daydreams or at night)
NCERT key ideas: These changes happen slowly, over months and years, not at an exact age and not all at once — some people change early & quickly, others slowly. All of them are aspects of sexual maturation. Such changes act as signals that tell other individuals that sexual maturation is taking place — needed for mating. Remember: some sexual maturation does not mean the body or mind is ready for sexual acts or for bringing up children.
NCERT Section 7.3.3(d)

Reproductive Health & Contraception

Preventing unwanted pregnancies and sexually transmitted diseases (STDs). Remember — the sexual act always has the potential to lead to pregnancy, and pregnancy makes major demands on the body and mind.

Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)

The sexual act is a very intimate connection of bodies, so many diseases can be sexually transmitted. These include Bacterial infections (Gonorrhoea, Syphilis) and Viral infections (Warts, HIV-AIDS). Using a covering, called a condom, for the penis during sex helps to prevent transmission of many of these infections to some extent!

1. Mechanical Barrier

Creates a barrier so that sperm does not reach the egg.

  • Condoms (on penis / in vagina)
  • Only method that also protects against STDs!

2. Hormonal Pills

Change the hormonal balance so eggs are not released.

  • Oral pills
  • Can cause side-effects (hormone changes)

3. Intrauterine Devices

Placed in the uterus to prevent pregnancy.

  • Loop & Copper-T
  • Can irritate the uterus (side effects)
  • Does NOT protect against STDs

4. Surgical Methods

Blocks gamete transport; safe in the long run.

  • Vasectomy — blocks vas deferens (male)
  • Tubectomy — blocks fallopian tube (female)
  • If surgery is not done properly → infection risk

Social Aspects & Sex Ratio

Surgical methods can be misused for illegal sex-selective abortion of female foetuses. Because of reckless female foeticides, the child sex ratio is declining at an alarming rate in some sections of our society. Prenatal sex determination has been prohibited by law — a healthy society needs a maintained female-male sex ratio.

Interactive Experiments

NCERT Class 10 Biology Lab Vault

All 7 mandatory NCERT lab activities (7.1 to 7.7) — procedure + observation for practical exams.

Activity 7.1

Yeast Sugar Fermentation Culture

Dissolve 10 g sugar in 100 mL water; take 20 mL in a test tube + a pinch of yeast granules. Cotton plug, keep warm. After 1–2 hours, observe a drop under the microscope.

Observation: Yeast cells grow rapidly and put out small buds that separate into new individuals — budding!
Activity 7.2

Bread Mould (Rhizopus) Culture

Wet a slice of bread, keep in a cool, moist, dark place. Observe the surface with a magnifying glass; record for a week.

Observation: White thread-like hyphae (non-reproductive) and black blob-on-a-stick sporangia full of spores. Compare with yeast: mould grows as filaments, yeast as buds.
Activity 7.3

Amoeba Binary Fission Slides

Observe a permanent slide of normal Amoeba vs a slide of Amoeba undergoing binary fission. Compare the two.

Observation: Karyokinesis (nucleus division) followed by cytokinesis (cytoplasm splitting) — division in any plane.
Activity 7.4

Spirogyra Pond Water Filaments

Collect dark green pond water with filamentous structures; mount filaments with a glycerine drop and coverslip.

Observation: Filamentous Spirogyra with spiral chloroplasts breaks into fragments upon maturation → new individuals.
Activity 7.5 & 7.6

Potato Eyes & Money Plant Cuttings

Cut potato so some pieces contain a notch/bud and some don't; place on wet cotton. Cut money-plant pieces with vs without a leaf node; dip in water.

Observation: Only pieces with buds/notches (potato) or nodes (money plant) give fresh shoots & roots — the bud is essential!
Activity 7.7

Bengal Gram (Chana) Germination

Soak gram seeds overnight; drain, cover with a wet cloth for a day (keep moist). Cut open carefully and compare with Fig 7.9.

Observation: Cotyledons store food; Plumule → future shoot; Radicle → future root.
