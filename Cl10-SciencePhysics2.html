Charge, Electric Current & Potential

The Base (Charge)

Charge is stated by the "deficiency" or excess of electrons in a Body.

If a body has deficiency of electrons it is said to be "Positively Charged"

If a body has excess of electrons it is said to be "Negatively Charged"

Its is denoted by Q

  • or
  • n = Number of electrons, e = clarge of an electron

    • SI Unit: coulomb (C)
    • Minimum charge of a body = Charge of 1 electron = 1.6 x 10^-19 C

The Flow (Current)

Electric current is expressed by the amount of charge flowing through a particular area in unit time. It is the rate of flow of electric charges.

I = Q / t
  • SI Unit: Ampere (A)
  • Measured by: Ammeter (connected in series)

The Push (Potential)

Electric potential difference between two points is the work done to move a unit charge from one point to the other.

V = W / Q
  • SI Unit: Volt (V)
  • Measured by: Voltmeter (connected in parallel)

V $\propto$ I

The potential difference, V, across the ends of a given metallic wire in an electric circuit is directly proportional to the current flowing through it, provided its temperature remains the same.

V = I × R

R is a constant called Resistance.

Heating Effect & Power

Joule's Law of Heating

Heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to the square of current (I²), resistance (R), and time (t).

H = I2Rt

Electric Power

Rate at which electric energy is dissipated in a circuit.

P = VI | P = I²R | P = V²/R

Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for filament of electric lamps?

Model Answer

Tungsten has a very high melting point (3380°C) and does not oxidize (burn) at high temperatures, allowing it to emit light when heated.
