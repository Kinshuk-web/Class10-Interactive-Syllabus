Chapter 8 · 60% of this page

Introduction to Trigonometry

Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the sides and angles of a triangle. In Class 10 we only need right-angled triangles — and everything in this chapter flows from one picture.

8.1 Why ratios? The idea in one diagram

Fix an angle θ in a right triangle. What matters is not the triangle's size — all right triangles with the same angle θ have the same shape (they are similar). So the ratio of any two sides depends only on θ, never on how big the triangle is. That is the whole trick: trigonometry replaces "which angle?" with "which ratio?".

The one thing to never forget
Name the sides relative to θ: the side across from θ is the opposite; the side helping form θ (not the hypotenuse) is the adjacent; the longest side (facing the right angle) is the hypotenuse. If you label sides correctly, every ratio in this chapter is just division.

8.2 The six trigonometric ratios

For a right triangle with angle θ, using P = perpendicular (opposite), B = base (adjacent) and H = hypotenuse:

primary ratios
sin θ = P / H cos θ = B / H tan θ = P / B
reciprocal ratios
cosec θ = H / P = 1/sin θ sec θ = H / B = 1/cos θ cot θ = B / P = 1/tan θ
quotient link
tan θ = sin θ / cos θ cot θ = cos θ / sin θ
Memory aid — a classic exam trap
SOH · CAH · TOA: Sin = Opposite / Hypotenuse, Cos = Adjacent / Hypotenuse, Tan = Opposite / Adjacent. And because the hypotenuse is the longest side, ratios of the form P/H and B/H can never exceed 1 — so 0 < sin θ < 1 and 0 < cos θ < 1 for an acute θ. A favourite MCQ asks whether sin θ = 4/3 is possible. It is not.

The √n trick — never memorise blindly again
Write the sine row as √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2. The cosine row is the same reversed. Tangent is just sine ÷ cosine: 0, 1/√3, 1, √3, undefined. At 0° and 90° the "undefined" entries happen because the needed side length is 0 — division by zero.

8.4 Complementary angles: sin(90° − θ) = cos θ

In a right triangle the two acute angles always add to 90°. Look at angle (90° − θ) and its opposite side becomes what was θ's adjacent side — so every ratio flips into its partner:

sin(90° − θ) = cos θ  ·  cos(90° − θ) = sin θ  ·  tan(90° − θ) = cot θ  ·  cosec(90° − θ) = sec θ  ·  sec(90° − θ) = cosec θ  ·  cot(90° − θ) = tan θ
Board pattern
"Express sin 67° + cos 75° in ratios of angles between 0° and 45°" becomes cos 23° + sin 15°. Apply the flip to anything above 45°, and remember tan 1° · tan 2° · … · tan 89° = 1 because pairs like tan 20°·tan 70° = tan 20°·cot 20° = 1.

8.5 Trigonometric identities

An identity holds for every value of θ. There are three core ones (the rest are derived from them):

identity 1 · Pythagorean
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
identity 2 · from ÷ cos²θ
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
identity 3 · from ÷ sin²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

Divide identity 1 by cos²θ and you get identity 2; divide it by sin²θ and you get identity 3 — one fact, three costumes. Try to break them: the machine below computes both sides independently for whatever θ you choose.

Featured proof — the identity that fooled a first attempt

A much-asked identity (and a nice story):

(1 + sinθ − cosθ)² / (1 + sinθ + cosθ)² = (1 − cosθ) / (1 + cosθ)

Watch the sign order — with (1 − sinθ + cosθ) on the left the statement is false (test θ = 30°: left ≈ 0.333, right ≈ 0.072). The proof works on the LHS alone:

  1. Multiply numerator and denominator by (1 + cosθ)² — this changes nothing, but sets up the key expansion.
  2. Expand (1 + sinθ − cosθ)(1 + cosθ) = 1 + sinθ + sinθcosθ − cos²θ, then replace 1 − cos²θ with sin²θ to get sinθ · (1 + sinθ + cosθ).
  3. The factor (1 + sinθ + cosθ)² now appears on top and bottom — cancel it, leaving sin²θ / (1 + cosθ)².
  4. Write sin²θ = (1 − cosθ)(1 + cosθ) and cancel one factor: (1 − cosθ)/(1 + cosθ). Done — LHS became RHS.

You can watch both sides stay equal for every θ in Module 3 above (last identity in the dropdown).

Chapter 9 · 40% of this page

Some Applications of Trigonometry

Chapter 8 taught you the ratios. Chapter 9 uses exactly one of them at a time to measure things you cannot reach — towers, kites, ships — using nothing but an angle and one distance.

9.1 The two vocabulary words

looking UP
Angle of elevation — the angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line drawn up to the object. Observer looks up at a tower.
looking DOWN
Angle of depression — the angle between the horizontal and the line drawn down to the object. Observer looks down from a height.
The single most useful fact in Chapter 9
The angle of depression from the top of a tower to a point on the ground equals the angle of elevation from that point to the top — they are alternate interior angles between the two parallel horizontals. So every "depression" problem secretly becomes an "elevation" problem. Also: heights and distances in this chapter always assume the object's base is on the same horizontal level as the observer unless stated otherwise.

9.2 The 4-step recipe for every Chapter 9 problem

  1. Draw the triangle. Put the observer, the object, the right angle at the base, and mark the given angle. 80% of mistakes are drawing mistakes.
  2. Mark what you know — one angle is always given; one side is always given (or implied, like "moved 20 m closer").
  3. Choose the ratio that connects them. Two knowns, one unknown: if the unknown is opposite and you know the adjacent, pick tan. If you know the hypotenuse (a string, a wire, a ladder), pick sin or cos.
  4. Solve and sanity-check. Heights come out positive and "look" right. Round only at the end. √3 ≈ 1.732 if a decimal is needed.

9.3 Worked patterns you must recognise

when the question says…the hidden equationwhy
"a 20 m wire to the top makes 30° with ground"sin 30° = h/20the wire is the hypotenuse
"elevation of top is 30° from 30 m away"tan 30° = h/30distance on ground is adjacent
"depression of car is 30° from a 7 m building"tan 30° = 7/ddepression = elevation (alternate angles)
"on moving 20 m towards the tower it becomes 60°"h = √3·d and h = (d+20)/√3two triangles, one common height — solve simultaneously
"two depression angles, 30° and 45°, same side"d₂ = h·cot30° − h·cot45°subtract the two base distances
Numbers worth knowing cold
sin30 = cos60 = 1/2, tan30 = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577, tan45 = 1, tan60 = √3 ≈ 1.732, sin60 = cos30 = √3/2. Chapter 9 answers are almost always of the form 10√3, 50√3, 12+4√3… built from these.

A Chapter 8 — NCERT solved

Ch 8Ex 8.1 Q13 marks
In ΔABC, right-angled at B, AB = 24 cm and BC = 7 cm. Find sin A, cos A, sin C, cos C.
Show solution

First the hypotenuse: AC = √(24² + 7²) = √(576+49) = √625 = 25 cm.

For angle A: opposite = BC = 7, adjacent = AB = 24, hypotenuse = 25 → sin A = 7/25, cos A = 24/25.

For angle C the roles of AB and BC swap (C's opposite is AB): sin C = 24/25, cos C = 7/25.

sin A = 7/25, cos A = 24/25, sin C = 24/25, cos C = 7/25

Notice sin A = cos C — that is the complementary-angle identity at work: A + C = 90°.

Ch 8Ex 8.1 Q22 marks
In ΔABC right-angled at B, given 15 cot A = 8. Find sin A and sec A.
Show solution

cot A = AB/BC = 8/15, so take AB = 8k, BC = 15k.

AC = √(64k² + 225k²) = √289k² = 17k.

sin A = BC/AC = 15/17  ·  sec A = AC/AB = 17/8.

sin A = 15/17, sec A = 17/8

Ch 8Ex 8.1 Q43 marks
Given sec θ = 13/12, calculate all other trigonometric ratios.
Show solution

cos θ = 12/13. Using sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ = 1 − 144/169 = 25/169:

sin θ = 5/13, then tan θ = sinθ/cosθ = 5/12, cot θ = 12/5, cosec θ = 13/5.

sin θ = 5/13 · cos θ = 12/13 · tan θ = 5/12 · cot θ = 12/5 · cosec θ = 13/5

If you spot the 5-12-13 Pythagorean triple instantly, you skip the algebra entirely.

Ch 8Ex 8.21 mark
Evaluate: (i) sin 60° cos 30° + sin 30° cos 60°   (ii) 2 tan²45° + cos²30° − sin²60°
Show solution

(i) (√3/2)(√3/2) + (1/2)(1/2) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1.

(ii) 2(1) + (√3/2)² − (√3/2)² = 2 + 0 = 2 — the last two terms cancel exactly.

(i) 1    (ii) 2

Ch 8Ex 8.3 Q1/Q21 mark each
Express in ratios of angles between 0° and 45°: (i) sin 67° + cos 75°   (ii) cot 85° + cos 75°
Show solution

(i) sin 67° = cos 23° and cos 75° = sin 15° → cos 23° + sin 15°.

(ii) cot 85° = tan 5° and cos 75° = sin 15° → tan 5° + sin 15°.

(i) cos 23° + sin 15°    (ii) tan 5° + sin 15°

Rule: anything above 45°, flip it with sin↔cos, tan↔cot, sec↔cosec using (90° − θ).

Ch 8Ex 8.3 Q42 marks
If tan (A+B) = √3 and tan (A−B) = 1/√3, A > B, find A and B.
Show solution

tan 60° = √3 → A + B = 60°.   tan 30° = 1/√3 → A − B = 30°.

Adding: 2A = 90° → A = 45°; subtracting: 2B = 30° → B = 15°.

A = 45°, B = 15°

Ch 8Ex 8.4 Q23 marks
Prove: (i) √[(1+sinθ)/(1−sinθ)] = sec θ + tan θ   (ii) √[(1−cosθ)/(1+cosθ)] = cosec θ − cot θ
Show solution

(i) Multiply inside the root by (1+sinθ)/(1+sinθ):

√[(1+sinθ)²/(1−sin²θ)] = (1+sinθ)/√(cos²θ) = (1+sinθ)/cosθ = secθ + tanθ

(ii) Same move with (1−cosθ):

√[(1−cosθ)²/(1−cos²θ)] = (1−cosθ)/sinθ = cosecθ − cotθ

Both proved — LHS = RHS

Ch 8Ex 8.4 Q54 marks
Prove: (sinθ + cosecθ)² + (cosθ + secθ)² = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ
Show solution

Expand both squares:

sin²θ + 2 sinθ·cosecθ + cosec²θ + cos²θ + 2 cosθ·secθ + sec²θ

2 sinθ·cosecθ = 2 and 2 cosθ·secθ = 2; group the rest: (sin²θ + cos²θ) = 1.

So far: 1 + 2 + 2 + cosec²θ + sec²θ = 5 + cosec²θ + sec²θ.

Now cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ and sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ:

5 + 1 + cot²θ + 1 + tan²θ = 7 + tan²θ + cot²θ. ∎

B Chapter 8 — other important solved

Ch 8practice3 marks
If tan θ = √3, evaluate (sinθ + cosθ)/(sinθ − cosθ).
Show solution

tan θ = √3 → θ = 60° → sin θ = √3/2, cos θ = 1/2.

(√3/2 + 1/2)/(√3/2 − 1/2) = (√3+1)/(√3−1); rationalise by multiplying by (√3+1)/(√3+1):

(√3+1)²/(3−1) = (4+2√3)/2 = 2+√3 ≈ 3.73.

2 + √3

Ch 8practice3 marks
If sin θ + cos θ = √2, find the value of sin θ · cos θ.
Show solution

Square both sides: sin²θ + 2 sinθ cosθ + cos²θ = 2.

Since sin²θ + cos²θ = 1: 1 + 2 sinθcosθ = 2 → sinθcosθ = 1/2 (so θ = 45°).

sin θ · cos θ = 1/2

Ch 8practice3 marks
Prove: (cosecθ − sinθ)(secθ − cosθ) = 1/(tanθ + cotθ).
Show solution

LHS: [(1−sin²θ)/sinθ] · [(1−cos²θ)/cosθ] = (cos²θ · sin²θ)/(sinθcosθ) = sinθ cosθ.

RHS: 1/[(sinθ/cosθ) + (cosθ/sinθ)] = (sinθcosθ)/(sin²θ+cos²θ) = sinθ cosθ.

LHS = RHS ∎

Ch 8practice2 marks
If sin(A−B) = 1/2 and cos(A+B) = 1/2, where A, B are acute with A > B, find A and B.
Show solution

sin 30° = 1/2 → A−B = 30°; cos 60° = 1/2 → A+B = 60°.

Adding: 2A = 90° → A = 45°, so B = 15°.

A = 45°, B = 15°

C Chapter 9 — NCERT solved

Ch 9Ex 9.12 marks
A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope tightly tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground, making 30° with the ground. Find the height of the pole.
Show solution

The rope is the hypotenuse; the pole is the side opposite the 30° angle.

sin 30° = h/20 → h = 20 × 1/2 = 10 m.

Height of pole = 10 m

Ch 9Ex 9.13 marks
A tree breaks due to storm and the broken part bends so that the top touches the ground making an angle of 30° with it. The distance between the foot of the tree and the point where the top touches the ground is 8 m. Find the height of the tree.
Show solution

Let the upright part be x and the broken (slanted) part be y.

tan 30° = x/8 → x = 8/√3  ·  cos 30° = 8/y → y = 8/(√3/2) = 16/√3.

Height of tree = x + y = 24/√3 = 8√3 ≈ 13.86 m.

Height of tree = 8√3 m (≈ 13.86 m)

Ch 9Ex 9.12 marks
The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground, which is 30 m away from the foot of the tower, is 30°. Find the height of the tower.
Show solution

tan 30° = h/30 → h = 30/√3 = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m.

Height of tower = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m

Ch 9Ex 9.13 marks
A statue 1.6 m tall stands on the top of a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is 60° and the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is 45°. Find the height of the pedestal.
Show solution

Let pedestal height = h and horizontal distance = d.

Pedestal triangle: tan 45° = h/d → d = h.

Full-height triangle: tan 60° = (h + 1.6)/d = (h+1.6)/h, so √3·h = h + 1.6.

h(√3 − 1) = 1.6 → h = 1.6/(√3−1) × (√3+1)/(√3+1) = 1.6(√3+1)/2 = 0.8(√3+1) ≈ 2.19 m.

Height of pedestal = 0.8(√3 + 1) m ≈ 2.19 m

Ch 9Ex 9.1 Q114 marks
A TV tower stands vertically on a bank of a canal. From a point on the other bank directly opposite the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 60°. From another point 20 m away from this point on the line joining this point to the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 30°. Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.
Show solution

Let width = w, height = h. Near point gives tan 60° = h/w → h = √3·w.

Far point (w + 20 from base): tan 30° = h/(w+20) → h = (w+20)/√3.

Equate: √3w = (w+20)/√3 → 3w = w + 20 → w = 10, so h = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m.

Width of canal = 10 m · Height of tower = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m

Ch 9Ex 9.1 Q134 marks
As observed from the top of a 75 m high lighthouse from the sea-level, the angles of depression of two ships are 30° and 45°. If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side, find the distance between the two ships.
Show solution

Depression angles equal elevation angles at the ships (alternate interior angles).

Nearer ship (45°): tan 45° = 75/d₁ → d₁ = 75 m.

Farther ship (30°): tan 30° = 75/d₂ → d₂ = 75√3 m.

Gap: d₂ − d₁ = 75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) ≈ 54.90 m.

Distance between ships = 75(√3 − 1) m ≈ 54.90 m

D Chapter 9 — other important solved

Ch 9practice3 marks
Two men are on opposite sides of a tower. They observe the angles of elevation of the top of the tower as 30° and 60°. If the height of the tower is 10√3 m, find the distance between the men.
Show solution

d₁ = h·cot 60° = 10√3/√3 = 10 m; d₂ = h·cot 30° = 10√3·√3 = 30 m.

Men are on opposite sides: total distance = 10 + 30 = 40 m.

40 m

Ch 9practice3 marks
The shadow of a tower, when the angle of elevation of the sun is 30°, is found to be 10 m longer than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.
Show solution

Let height h, shorter shadow x: tan 60° = h/x → h = √3x; longer shadow: tan 30° = h/(x+10) → h = (x+10)/√3.

√3x = (x+10)/√3 → 3x = x + 10 → x = 5, so h = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 m.

Height = 5√3 m ≈ 8.66 m

Ch 8CBSE 20201 markMCQ
If tan θ = cot (30° + θ), then θ equals: (a) 15° (b) 30° (c) 45° (d) 60°
Show solution

cot(30°+θ) = tan(90° − 30° − θ) = tan(60° − θ).

So tan θ = tan(60° − θ) → θ = 60° − θ → θ = 15°.

(a) 15°

Ch 8CBSE 20201 markMCQ
The value of tan 1° · tan 2° · tan 3° · … · tan 89° is: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 1/2 (d) not defined
Show solution

Pair terms from both ends: tan θ · tan(90°−θ) = tan θ · cot θ = 1. Every pair multiplies to 1, and the middle term is tan 45° = 1.

(b) 1

Ch 8CBSE 20192 marks
If cos θ = 7/25, find the value of tan θ.
Show solution

sin θ = √(1 − 49/625) = √(576/625) = 24/25.

tan θ = sinθ/cosθ = (24/25)/(7/25) = 24/7.

tan θ = 24/7

The 7-24-25 triple — worth memorising alongside 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17.

Ch 8CBSE 20163 marks
If x = 3 sin θ + 4 cos θ and y = 3 cos θ − 4 sin θ, prove that x² + y² = 25.
Show solution

x² = 9sin²θ + 24 sinθcosθ + 16cos²θ

y² = 9cos²θ − 24 sinθcosθ + 16sin²θ

Adding, the cross terms cancel: x² + y² = 9(sin²+cos²) + 16(sin²+cos²) = 9 + 16 = 25 ∎

Ch 8CBSE 2020 (Standard)3 marks
Prove: (1 + cot θ − cosec θ)(1 + tan θ + sec θ) = 2.
Show solution

Write everything over sin/cos. First bracket: 1 + cosθ/sinθ − 1/sinθ = (sinθ + cosθ − 1)/sinθ.

Second bracket: (cosθ + sinθ + 1)/cosθ.

Product: [(sinθ+cosθ) − 1][(sinθ+cosθ) + 1]/(sinθcosθ) = [(sinθ+cosθ)² − 1]/(sinθcosθ)

= (1 + 2sinθcosθ − 1)/(sinθcosθ) = 2 ∎

If the height of a vertical pole is equal to the length of its shadow on the ground, the angle of elevation of the sun is: (a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 90°

Ch 9CBSE 20182 marks
A ladder makes an angle of 60° with the ground when placed against a wall. If the foot of the ladder is 2 m away from the wall, find the length of the ladder.
Show solution

Ch 9CBSE 20193 marks
From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed on top of a 20 m high building are 45° and 60° respectively. Find the height of the tower.
Show solution

Let tower height t, distance d. Building triangle: tan 45° = 20/d → d = 20.

Whole height triangle: tan 60° = (20+t)/20 → 20√3 = 20 + t.

t = 20(√3 − 1) ≈ 14.64 m.

Height of tower = 20(√3 − 1) m ≈ 14.64 m

Ch 9CBSE 20194 marks
The angles of depression of the top and the bottom of an 8 m tall building from the top of a multi-storeyed tower are 30° and 45° respectively. Find the height of the tower. (Take √3 = 1.732)
Show solution

Let tower height H, horizontal distance d. Bottom of building: tan 45° = H/d → d = H.

Top of building (H − 8 above ground): tan 30° = (H−8)/d = (H−8)/H.

H/√3 = H − 8 → H = √3H − 8√3 → H(√3−1) = 8√3 → H = 8√3/(√3−1).

Rationalise: H = 8√3(√3+1)/2 = 4√3(√3+1) = 12 + 4√3 ≈ 18.93 m.

Height of tower = (12 + 4√3) m ≈ 18.93 m
