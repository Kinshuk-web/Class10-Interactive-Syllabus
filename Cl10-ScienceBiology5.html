Section 13.1

Ecosystem — what are its components?

All interacting organisms in an area together with the non-living surroundings form an ecosystem. Tap anything in the pond below.

A pond ecosystem — tap a component biotic vs abiotic
Tap a component

Biotic = living (plants, animals, microbes). Abiotic = physical factors (temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, minerals).

Producers

Make organic compounds (sugar, starch) from inorganic substances using the radiant energy of the Sun. All green plants and some bacteria.

Consumers

Consume food produced by producers, directly or indirectly. Herbivores, carnivores, omnivores and parasites.

Decomposers

Bacteria and fungi that break down dead remains and waste into simple inorganic substances, which return to the soil and are reused by plants.

Natural vs human-made ecosystems
Natural

Forests, ponds, lakes

Artificial

Gardens, crop-fields, aquarium

Why an aquarium needs cleaning

A pond is self-sustaining because decomposers recycle nutrients continuously. An aquarium has too few decomposers and no soil link, so waste accumulates — it must be cleaned, while a pond is cleaned by its own microbes.

Trophic levels — the pyramid
The rules that matter
  • Green plants capture about 1% of the sunlight falling on their leaves and convert it to food energy.
  • On average only 10% of the food eaten becomes the consumer's own body — the rest is lost as heat, used in digestion and work.
  • Because so little passes on, food chains have only 3–4 steps.
  • Numbers are generally greatest at the lowest trophic level — the producers.
  • Energy flow is unidirectional: it never returns to the Sun or to the previous level.

Section 13.1.1

Biological magnification

Pesticides are non-degradable — so they don't disappear. They get concentrated at every step. Drag the slider.

Concentration calculator start: 0.02 ppm in water
PlanktonSmall fishLarge fishHuman
How it enters

Pesticides sprayed on crops wash into soil and water bodies; plants absorb them with water and minerals, aquatic organisms take them up from water.

Why it accumulates

These chemicals are not degradable, so the body cannot break or excrete them. Each consumer eats many organisms below it, so the dose piles up.

Why we get the most

Humans occupy the top of most food chains, so the maximum concentration collects in our bodies — and washing does not always remove it from grain, vegetables or meat.

Section 13.2.1

The ozone layer — our UV shield

Ozone (O₃) high in the atmosphere blocks harmful UV radiation. Switch on CFC pollution and watch what happens.

Atmosphere simulator
Layer intact

Ozone at higher levels of the atmosphere shields the surface from UV radiation — radiation that is highly damaging to organisms and is known to cause skin cancer in humans.

How ozone forms — and why it's a paradox
O₂ —UV→ O + O
O + O₂ → O₃ (ozone)
  • Ozone is made of three oxygen atoms.
  • At higher levels of the atmosphere it is essential — it absorbs UV.
  • At ground level it is a deadly poison.
  • It is a product of UV radiation acting on molecular oxygen.
Depletion & the global response
  • Ozone amount began dropping sharply in the 1980s.
  • Linked to synthetic chemicals like chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.
  • In 1987, the UNEP succeeded in an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.
  • It is now mandatory for manufacturers to make CFC-free refrigerators worldwide.
Section 13.2.2

Managing the garbage we produce

Why does a plastic bag outlive us while a banana peel disappears? Because enzymes are specific.

Sort the waste — tap an item score: 0/12
biodegradable non-biodegradable
Biodegradable

Broken down by biological processes — bacteria and other saprophytes act on them. Examples: food waste, paper, cotton, wood, leather, cow dung.

In a pit, these change form and structure over weeks — microbes convert them into simple inorganic substances that enrich the soil.
Non-biodegradable

Not broken down by biological processes. They may be inert and simply persist for a long time, or actively harm members of the ecosystem. Examples: plastics, metals, glass, DDT, CFCs.

Enzymes are specific — that's why we get no energy from eating coal, and why microbes cannot digest plastic. Such materials are only acted on by heat and pressure.

In-text & exercise questions
Each step or level of a food chain is a trophic level. Example: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake. Grass = first trophic level (producers), grasshopper = second (primary consumer/herbivore), frog = third (secondary consumer), snake = fourth (tertiary consumer).
They break down the complex organic substances in dead remains and waste products into simple inorganic substances that go into the soil and are used again by plants — so nutrients are recycled and the soil is replenished.
Enzymes are specific in their action — a particular enzyme is needed to break a particular substance. Microbes have enzymes for natural materials (food, paper, cotton) but not for human-made ones like plastics, so those are not broken down biologically and persist.
The progressive accumulation of non-degradable chemicals at each trophic level is biological magnification. Yes — the concentration increases at each successive level, being maximum at the top of the food chain (humans).
The chain breaks. Organisms of the level above lose their food and may die or migrate; organisms below may multiply unchecked. The imbalance spreads through the whole ecosystem because the components are interdependent. Removing producers is the most damaging, since all energy enters through them.
They persist in the environment for very long periods, clog drains and soil, harm animals that swallow them, reduce soil fertility and water percolation, and — when they carry chemicals — enter food chains and magnify. Burning them releases toxic gases.
The ozone layer absorbs UV radiation, which is highly damaging — it causes skin cancer in humans and harms crops and other organisms. Steps: in 1987 the UNEP got an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels, and it is now mandatory to make CFC-free refrigerators worldwide.
Board-style questions
(i) Energy captured by autotrophs does not revert to the solar input, and energy passed to herbivores does not return to autotrophs. (ii) As it moves progressively through trophic levels it is no longer available to the previous level, and the energy available at each level diminishes because of loss at every step.
Because only about 10% of the energy at one level is transferred to the next. The loss at each step is so great that after three or four levels very little usable energy remains to support another level.
A pond is a natural ecosystem with producers, consumers and decomposers plus soil, so nutrients are recycled continuously and it sustains itself. An aquarium is human-made and has few decomposers and no soil link, so waste accumulates and it must be cleaned periodically; oxygen and food must also be supplied.
Pesticides washed into water are taken up by aquatic plants and animals. Being non-degradable they are not excreted, so they accumulate progressively: water (very low) → plankton → small fish → large fish → fish-eating birds/humans (highest). Since humans occupy the top of most food chains, the maximum concentration collects in our bodies — which is why grains, vegetables, fruit and meat carry pesticide residues that washing cannot always remove.
(i) Segregate waste at home into biodegradable and non-biodegradable and compost the biodegradable part. (ii) Reduce and reuse — carry a cloth bag, avoid disposable plastic, buy only what is needed, and give paper/glass/metal for recycling.
