Circles — tangents, and nothing else

In Class 10, "Circles" means one thing: the tangent — the line that touches a circle at exactly one point. Everything in this chapter (both theorems, every exam question) follows from the two facts you will see in the interactive modules below.

9.1 Vocabulary first: secant, tangent, point of contact

crosses twice
Secant — a line that intersects the circle at two points. It slices through the circle, cutting off a chord.
touches once
Tangent — a line that meets the circle at exactly one point. That single common point is the point of contact. A tangent is what a secant becomes as the two intersection points slide together.

How many tangents can a circle have? Infinitely many — one at every point of the circle. But from any single external point, exactly two tangents can be drawn. The count depends on where the point stands:

outside the circle (d > r)2symmetric tangents on either side of the line OP

9.2 Theorem 1 — the tangent is perpendicular to the radius

Theorem: the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

Proof (the "shortest distance" argument): Let the tangent at P have centre O. Take any other point Q on the tangent. Since the tangent meets the circle only at P, Q lies outside the circle, so OQ > r while OP = r. So P is the point on the tangent closest to O — and the shortest distance from a point to a line is always the perpendicular one. Hence OP ⊥ tangent. ∎

Converse (also asked in exams): the line drawn through the point of contact, perpendicular to the tangent, must pass through the centre.

9.3 Theorem 2 — equal tangents from an external point

Theorem: the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.

Proof (RHS congruence): PA and PB are tangents from P, touching at A and B. Join OP, OA, OB. By Theorem 1, ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°. Now OP is common, and OA = OB (radii). So ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (RHS rule) → PA = PB. ∎

A NCERT solved

CirclesEx Q1 mark
How many tangents can a circle have?
Show solution

A tangent exists at every point of the circle, and there are infinitely many points on a circle — so a circle has infinitely many tangents.

But note the examiner's twist: from a single external point, exactly two tangents can be drawn; from a point on the circle, exactly one.

Infinitely many — one at each point of the circle

CirclesEx Q1 mark each
Fill in the blanks: (i) A tangent to a circle intersects it in ___ point(s). (ii) A line intersecting a circle in two points is called a ___. (iii) A circle can have ___ parallel tangents at the most to a given line. (iv) The common point of a tangent and the circle is called the ___.
Show solution

(i) exactly one   (ii) secant   (iii) two (one on each side of the circle, both perpendicular to the diameter)   (iv) point of contact.

one · secant · two · point of contact

CirclesEx Q2 marks
A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at a point Q so that OQ = 12 cm. Find the length of PQ.
Show solution

By Theorem 1, ∠OPQ = 90° — so ΔOPQ is right-angled at P.

PQ = √(OQ² − OP²) = √(12² − 5²) = √(144 − 25) = √119 ≈ 10.91 cm

PQ = √119 cm ≈ 10.91 cm

5-12-13 triple "broken": the 13 here is OQ, not PQ. Watch which side the hypotenuse is on.

CirclesEx Q2 marks
From a point Q, the length of the tangent to a circle is 24 cm and the distance of Q from the centre is 25 cm. Find the radius of the circle.
Show solution

Right triangle with hypotenuse OQ = 25 (distance from centre) and one leg = 24 (tangent):

r = √(25² − 24²) = √(625 − 576) = √49 = 7 cm

Radius = 7 cm

CirclesEx Q2 marks
Two tangents PA and PB are drawn from an external point P to a circle with centre O, inclined to each other at an angle of 80°. Find ∠POA.
Show solution

By Theorem 2, OP bisects ∠APB: ∠OPA = 80°/2 = 40°. By Theorem 1, ∠OAP = 90°.

So ∠POA = 180° − 90° − 40° = 50°.

∠POA = 50°

Companion facts: ∠AOB = 2 × 50° = 100° = 180° − 80°, and ∠OAP + ∠OBP = 180°, so OAPB is cyclic.

CirclesEx Q2 marks
Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.
Show solution

Let PQ be a diameter with tangents l at P and m at Q.

By Theorem 1: l ⊥ OP and m ⊥ OQ.

Since P, O, Q are collinear, OP and OQ are the same line — so l and m are both perpendicular to the same straight line.

Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel → l ∥ m ∎

CirclesEx Q2 marks
Prove that the perpendicular at the point of contact to the tangent to a circle passes through the centre.
Show solution

Let the tangent at P be l, and let n be the line through P perpendicular to l. Suppose n does not pass through the centre O; then the radius OP is some other line through P.

But OP ⊥ l (Theorem 1), and n ⊥ l — through the same point P there would be two distinct perpendiculars to l, which is impossible.

Hence n coincides with OP and passes through O ∎

CirclesEx Q2 marks
Two concentric circles are of radii 5 cm and 3 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.
Show solution

Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at P. Then OP ⊥ AB (Theorem 1), so OP is the perpendicular from the centre to the chord — which bisects it.

AB = 2·AP = 2√(OA² − OP²) = 2√(25 − 9) = 2×4 = 8 cm

Chord = 8 cm

Bonus fact worth writing in the exam: P is the midpoint of AB — the chord is bisected at the point of contact.

CirclesEx Q3 marks
A quadrilateral ABCD is drawn to circumscribe a circle. Prove that AB + CD = AD + BC.
Show solution

Let the circle touch AB, BC, CD, DA at P, Q, R, S respectively.

Equal tangents from each vertex (Theorem 2): AP = AS, BP = BQ, CR = CQ, DR = DS.

Add: AB + CD = (AP+PB) + (CR+RD) and AD + BC = (AS+DS) + (BQ+CQ). Substituting the equal pairs term by term makes the two sides identical.

AB + CD = AD + BC ∎

CirclesEx Q3 marks
Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
Show solution

Let parallelogram ABCD circumscribe a circle. By the previous result: AB + CD = AD + BC.

In a parallelogram opposite sides are equal: AB = CD and AD = BC.

So 2AB = 2AD → AB = AD — and a parallelogram with adjacent sides equal has all four sides equal.

ABCD is a rhombus ∎

CirclesEx Q4 marks
A triangle ABC is drawn to circumscribe a circle of radius 4 cm, such that the segments BD and DC, into which BC is divided by the point of contact, are of lengths 8 cm and 6 cm respectively. Find the sides AB and AC.
Show solution

Let the circle touch BC at D, AB at F, AC at E. Equal tangents: BD = BF = 8, CD = CE = 6, AF = AE = x.

Sides: a = BC = 14, b = CA = x + 6, c = AB = x + 8, so s = x + 14.

Area two ways — rs (r = 4) and Heron's formula:

4(x+14) = √[(x+14) · x · 8 · 6] → 16(x+14)² = 48x(x+14) → 3x = x + 14 → x = 7

AB = x + 8 = 15 cm · AC = x + 6 = 13 cm

CirclesEx Q3 marks
Prove that the angle between the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line segment joining the points of contact at the centre.
Show solution

Tangents PA and PB from P touch at A and B. In quadrilateral OAPB:

∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (Theorem 1) → ∠OAP + ∠OBP = 180°.

Angles of a quadrilateral sum to 360°: ∠AOB + ∠APB = 360° − 180° = 180°.

∠AOB + ∠APB = 180° ∎  (this also proves OAPB is cyclic)

CirclesEx Q4 marks
XY and X′Y′ are two parallel tangents to a circle with centre O, and a third tangent AB, with point of contact C, intersects XY at A and X′Y′ at B. Prove that ∠AOB = 90°.
Show solution

Let XY touch the circle at P and X′Y′ at Q — so OP ⊥ XY, OQ ⊥ X′Y′, and P, O, Q are collinear (a diameter).

From A there are two tangents (AP and AC): by Theorem 2, OA bisects the angle between them → ∠OAC = ∠OAP. Similarly from B: ∠OBC = ∠OBQ.

In right ΔOAP: ∠OAP = 90° − ∠AOP. In right ΔOBQ: ∠OBQ = 90° − ∠BOQ.

In ΔAOB: ∠AOB = 180° − (∠OAC + ∠OBC) = (∠AOP + ∠BOQ).

But along the diameter PQ: ∠AOP + ∠AOB + ∠BOQ = 180°. Substituting: ∠AOB + ∠AOB = 180°.

∠AOB = 90° ∎

Circlespractice1 mark
TP and TQ are two tangents to a circle with centre O so that ∠POQ = 110°. Find ∠PTQ.
Show solution

In quadrilateral OPTQ: ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90° (Theorem 1).

∠PTQ = 360° − 90° − 90° − 110° = 70°.

∠PTQ = 70°

Same 3-second pattern for any data: ∠PTQ = 180° − ∠POQ. This exact question has appeared as a board MCQ.

Circlespractice2 marks
The angle between two tangents from an external point to a circle of radius r is 60°. Find the length of each tangent. If instead each tangent has length 5 cm, find r.
Show solution

OP bisects the 60° angle: ∠OPA = 30°, with the right angle at A.

tan 30° = r / PA → PA = r√3.

Conversely, if PA = 5: r = PA/√3 = 5/√3 ≈ 2.89 cm.

Each tangent = r√3 · and r = 5/√3 ≈ 2.89 cm

Circlespractice2 marks
A quadrilateral ABCD circumscribes a circle, with AB = 6 cm, BC = 7 cm and CD = 8 cm. Find AD.
Show solution

AB + CD = AD + BC → 6 + 8 = AD + 7.

AD = 7 cm

CirclesCBSE 20193 marks
Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
Show solution

Let l be the tangent at P, O the centre. Take any point Q ≠ P on l.

Since l meets the circle only at P, Q lies outside the circle → OQ > OP = r. So P is the point of l nearest to O.

The shortest segment from a point to a line is the perpendicular one → OP ⊥ l ∎

This is Theorem 1 verbatim — memorise the "every other point of the tangent lies outside the circle" phrasing; it earns the marks.

CirclesCBSE 20193 marks
Prove that the lengths of the tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
Show solution

Tangents PA and PB from P. Join OP, OA, OB.

∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (Theorem 1); OA = OB (radii); OP common.

ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (RHS) → PA = PB ∎

CirclesCBSE 20203 marks
Prove that opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the centre of the circle.
Show solution

Let ABCD circumscribe the circle, touching at P, Q, R, S. From each vertex, the two tangents are equal, so the line from the vertex to O bisects the angle there: e.g. ∠OAB = ∠OAD = ½∠A.

In ΔAOB: ∠AOB = 180° − (∠OAB + ∠OBA) = 180° − ½(∠A + ∠B).

In ΔCOD: ∠COD = 180° − ½(∠C + ∠D).

Adding: ∠AOB + ∠COD = 360° − ½(∠A+∠B+∠C+∠D) = 360° − ½(360°) = 180° ∎

CirclesCBSE 20164 marks
Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
Show solution

As proved in the NCERT section: tangent segments give AB + CD = AD + BC; the parallelogram gives AB = CD, AD = BC.

So 2AB = 2AD → AB = AD → all sides equal.

Hence ABCD is a rhombus ∎

CirclesCBSE 20172 marks

Show solution

CirclesCBSE 20182 marks
From an external point P, two tangents PA and PB are drawn to a circle with centre O. Prove that the quadrilateral OAPB is a cyclic quadrilateral.
Show solution

∠OAP = 90° and ∠OBP = 90° (Theorem 1).

Opposite angles: ∠OAP + ∠OBP = 180° — a quadrilateral with a pair of opposite angles supplementary is cyclic.

OAPB is cyclic ∎

CirclesCBSE 20171 markMCQ
Two tangents PA and PB are drawn from an external point P to a circle with centre O. If ∠APB = 60°, then ∠OAB equals: (a) 15° (b) 30° (c) 45° (d) 60°
Show solution

OP bisects ∠APB → ∠OPA = 30°; in right ΔOAP (right at A): ∠POA = 60°.

OA = OB and PA = PB → ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP → ∠AOB = 2×60° = 120°. In isosceles ΔOAB: ∠OAB = (180° − 120°)/2 = 30°.

(b) 30°
