4.3 · Method two

04Completing the square

Rewrite ax² + bx + c as a perfect square plus a leftover. Then one square root finishes the job — and this is exactly how the quadratic formula is born.

Step-through · 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0
The book's Example 5, one line at a time.
Divide by a. x² − (5/2)x + 3/2 = 0.
Move the constant. x² − (5/2)x = −3/2.
Add the magic number. Half of 5/2 is 5/4; its square is 25/16. Add to both sides: x² − (5/2)x + 25/16 = −3/2 + 25/16.
Both sides tidy up. (x − 5/4)² = 1/16.
Square root. x − 5/4 = ±1/4, so x = 3/2 or x = 1.
x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a

Doing the same completing-the-square steps with letters instead of numbers produces this formula. You never have to derive it in the exam, but knowing where it comes from makes it stick.

Worked example

06The train problem

A train covers 480 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 8 km/h less, it would have taken 3 hours more. Find the speed.

Step-through · a real quadratic
Time = distance ÷ speed, twice, then subtract.
Name it. Let the speed be x km/h. Then the usual time is 480/x hours.
Slower case. At (x − 8) km/h the time is 480/(x − 8).
The condition. 480/(x − 8) − 480/x = 3.
Clear the fractions. 480x − 480(x − 8) = 3x(x − 8) → 3840 = 3x² − 24x → x² − 8x − 1280 = 0.
Factorise. (x − 40)(x + 32) = 0 → x = 40 or x = −32.
Reject the negative. Speed cannot be negative, so the speed is 40 km/h.
Always check both roots Word problems often produce a second root that is mathematically correct but physically impossible — a negative speed, a negative age, a negative length. Say clearly why you reject it.

Before the exam

08Four traps

01

Divide by a first

Completing the square only works when the coefficient of x² is 1.

02

Two roots, not one

A quadratic has two roots (possibly equal). Stopping at one loses a mark.

03

Reject impossible answers

Negative lengths, ages and speeds are not answers — say why you discard them.

04

D tells you what to expect

Compute b² − 4ac first. If it is negative, stop — there are no real roots to find.

Chapter summary

14Five lines to revise

  • Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0.
  • Factorisation: split b into two parts that multiply to ac.
  • Formula: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/2a.
  • D > 0 two roots, D = 0 equal roots, D < 0 no real roots.
  • In word problems, reject roots that make no physical sense.
