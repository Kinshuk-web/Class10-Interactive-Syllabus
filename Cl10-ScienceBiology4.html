NCERT Section 8.1

Accumulation of Variation During Reproduction

Inheritance provides a common body design, but subtle inaccuracies during DNA copying generate variations that accumulate across generations.

Heredity: The transmission of characters, traits, and features from parents to offspring from one generation to the next.
Variation: The differences in characters or traits observed among individuals of the same species.
Asexual Reproduction: Generates very minor variations caused solely by small inaccuracies in DNA copying (e.g., sugarcane plants or reproducing bacteria).
Sexual Reproduction: Produces significant diversity because genetic material combines from two distinct parents.
Significance of Variation: Variations increase species adaptability and survival chances during environmental changes. For example, a population of bacteria in temperate waters with heat-resistant variants will survive a sudden heatwave!

NCERT Section 8.2

Mendel & Rules for Inheritance of Traits

Gregor Johann Mendel worked out the fundamental rules of inheritance using Garden Pea plants (Pisum sativum). Both parents contribute equal genetic material to the offspring.

Gregor Johann Mendel

Father of Modern Genetics

Austrian monk who blended science and mathematics to discover laws of inheritance through garden pea experiments (1856–1863).

Why Did Mendel Choose the Garden Pea (Pisum sativum)?

  • Clear Contrasting Traits: Easily distinguishable contrasting pairs (e.g., Tall/Short, Round/Wrinkled).
  • Short Life Cycle: Yields multiple generations in a short time frame.
  • Self & Cross Pollination: Naturally self-pollinating but easily cross-pollinated artificially.
  • Large Progeny Number: Produces numerous seeds per cross, yielding statistical accuracy.

NCERT Monohybrid Inheritance

Monohybrid Cross (Inheritance of One Trait)

A cross studying a single pair of contrasting characters (e.g., Plant Height: Tall vs Short).

Key Cross Steps (Tall x Short)

1. Parent Generation (P):

Pure Tall (TT) crossed with Pure Short (tt).

2. F1 Generation (First Filial):

All progeny plants are Tall (Genotype Tt). The trait that appears (Tall) is Dominant; the hidden trait (Short) is Recessive.

3. F2 Generation (Self-Pollination Tt x Tt):

The hidden dwarf/short trait reappears in F2 plants!

Standard F2 Ratios:
Phenotypic Ratio = 3 Tall : 1 Short (3:1)
Genotypic Ratio = 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1)

NCERT Postulates

Mendel's Laws of Inheritance

The three fundamental principles governing genetic inheritance in diploid organisms.

1

Law of Dominance

In a contrasting allele pair (heterozygous organism e.g., Tt), one allele expresses itself fully (Dominant allele 'T') while masking the other allele (Recessive allele 't').

F1 generation exhibits only the dominant phenotype.
2

Law of Segregation

During gamete formation, two alleles responsible for a character separate (segregate) so that each gamete carries only one allele for that trait (Purity of Gametes).

Explains why recessive traits reappear in F2.
3

Law of Independent Assortment

When two pairs of traits are combined in a hybrid cross, allele segregation of one pair is completely independent of allele segregation of the other pair.

Verified by the 9:3:3:1 dihybrid ratio.
NCERT Section 8.2.3

How do these Traits get Expressed?

Cellular DNA is the information source for making proteins in the cell. Genes control traits by producing specific enzymes that regulate biological pathways.

Molecular Mechanism of Trait Expression

Case Study: Plant Height Trait

Plant height depends on the quantity of a specific growth hormone (e.g., Gibberellin). The hormone amount depends on the efficiency of the enzyme making it!

  • Normal Dominant Gene (T): Encodes an efficient enzyme → High hormone synthesis → Plant grows Tall.
  • Altered Recessive Gene (t): Encodes an inefficient/less active enzyme → Low hormone synthesis → Plant remains Short.
Chromosomes in Gametes: Both parents contribute one chromosome of each homologous pair to the progeny, ensuring dual gene copies in diploid body cells!

NCERT Section 8.3

Sex Determination in Humans

In human beings, sex is determined genetically through sex chromosomes (23rd pair).

Chromosome Composition

  • 23 Pairs Total: Humans carry 23 pairs of chromosomes in body cell nuclei. 22 pairs are Autosomes (identical in males and females).
  • Females (XX): Carry a perfect pair of sex chromosomes (both X chromosomes). All eggs carry an X chromosome.
  • Males (XY): Carry a mismatched pair (one normal sized X and one short Y chromosome). 50% sperms carry X, and 50% carry Y.
Board Exam High-Yield Point: The sex of a child is determined by the father! If an X-bearing sperm fertilizes the egg, the child is female (XX). If a Y-bearing sperm fertilizes the egg, the child is male (XY).

Human Blood Group Inheritance (ABO System)

ABO blood groups in humans are controlled by gene I, which exists as three distinct alleles: IA, IB, and i.

Allele Dominance Rules

IA and IB are Co-dominant: Both alleles express simultaneously when present together, yielding Blood Group AB!
IA and IB are Dominant over allele i: Allele i is completely recessive (produces no antigen).

CBSE Board 2023 (3 Marks) Monohybrid Cross

Q1. A green stemmed rose plant denoted by GG and a brown stemmed rose plant denoted by gg are allowed to undergo a cross with each other. List your observations regarding stem color in F1 and F2 progeny.

Answer & Marking Scheme:
  • F1 Progeny: All plants will have Green stems (Genotype Gg), because Green stem (G) is dominant over Brown stem (g).
  • F2 Progeny (Gg x Gg): Phenotypic ratio will be 3 Green stemmed : 1 Brown stemmed (75% Green, 25% Brown). Genotypic ratio = 1 GG : 2 Gg : 1 gg.
CBSE Board 2022 (5 Marks Case Study) Sex Determination

Q2. "The sex of a newborn child is a matter of chance and none of the parents may be considered responsible for it." Justify this statement with a suitable flow chart.

Answer & Marking Scheme:

Human females produce all eggs carrying an X chromosome. Males produce 50% sperms with X and 50% sperms with Y chromosome. Fertilization by X-sperm gives a female child (XX) and Y-sperm gives a male child (XY). Hence, sex determination is purely a 50:50 genetic chance!
