Two Lines,
One Answer

Two equations with two unknowns describe two straight lines. Solving them means finding where the lines meet. Sometimes they meet once, sometimes never, and sometimes they are the same line wearing two different names.

3 possible endings2 algebraic methods19 NCERT questions
Every word problem in this chapter is the same sentence: “find the point where these two lines cross.”

3.2 · Graphical method

02Where do the two lines meet?

Each equation has infinitely many solutions — every point on its line. The pair's solution is the one point that satisfies both, which is where the lines cross.

03Three ratios, three endings

Lines intersect once. Exactly one solution. Consistent.

Parallel, never meeting. No solution. Inconsistent.

Case 3

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

Same line. Infinitely many solutions. Consistent (dependent).

3.3 · Algebraic methods

04Substitution and elimination

Graphs give you a picture but not always exact numbers. Algebra gives exact answers every time. Two methods do all the work in this chapter.

Substitution

Make one variable the subject of one equation, then put that expression into the other equation. You are left with one equation in one variable.

From (1): y = 5 − x
Put into (2): 2x − 3(5 − x) = 4
5x = 19 → x = 19/5

Elimination

Multiply the equations so that one variable has equal and opposite coefficients, then add. That variable disappears.

x + y = 5  (×3) → 3x + 3y = 15
2x − 3y = 4
Add: 5x = 19 → x = 19/5

Worked examples

05From words to equations

Example · The two-digit number

Step-through · Example 6
The sum of the digits is 9; nine times the number is twice its reversal.
Name the unknowns. Let the tens digit be x and the units digit be y, so the number is 10x + y.
First condition. x + y = 9.
Second condition. Reversed number is 10y + x, and 9(10x + y) = 2(10y + x).
Simplify. 90x + 9y = 20y + 2x → 88x = 11y → y = 8x.
Solve. x + 8x = 9 → x = 1, y = 8. The number is 18.

Example · The taxi fare

Step-through · fixed charge plus rate
₹105 for 10 km, ₹155 for 15 km.
Name the parts. Let the fixed charge be f and the charge per km be p.
Two equations. f + 10p = 105 and f + 15p = 155.
Subtract. 5p = 50, so p = 10.
Back-substitute. f = 105 − 100 = 5.
Answer the actual question. For 25 km: 5 + 25 × 10 = ₹255.

Before the exam

07Four traps

01

Signs when subtracting

Elimination by subtraction flips every sign in the second equation. Write the subtraction line out in full.

02

Consistent does not mean unique

A dependent pair is consistent — it has infinitely many solutions, not none.

03

Answer the question asked

Finding x and y is not the end. If the question asks for the fare for 25 km, substitute.

04

Ratios need standard form

Bring both equations to the form ax + by + c = 0 before comparing a, b, c.

Doubts, answered

10Frequently asked questions

One equation fixes a whole line of points — every point on it works. Adding a second equation asks for a point on both lines, and two different straight lines cross at only one point.

If one variable already has coefficient 1 or −1, substitute — it is fastest. If the coefficients are messy or large, eliminate. Both earn full marks; just show your steps.

Name what you are asked for with letters, then translate each sentence into an equation. “Sum is 9” becomes x + y = 9. Two sentences give two equations.

Always. Substitute x and y into the equation you did not use for solving. It takes ten seconds and catches almost every arithmetic slip.

Chapter summary

13Five lines to revise

  • a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → one solution (intersecting lines).
  • a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → no solution (parallel).
  • a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ → infinitely many (same line).
  • Substitution: express one variable, put it into the other equation.
  • Elimination: equalise a coefficient, then add or subtract.
