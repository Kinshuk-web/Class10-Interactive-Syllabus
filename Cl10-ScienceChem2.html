1. Core Chemical Concepts

Class 10 Board exams require understanding of how acids and bases behave in water, how they dissociate, and the fundamental differences between concentrated/dilute and strong/weak concepts.

Nature of Acids

Acids are chemical substances that release hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) or hydronium ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) when dissolved in water.

Dissociation:
$\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$

*$\text{H}^+$ cannot exist alone; it binds with water molecules.

  • ✔ Sour taste & corrosive to skin
  • ✔ Turn blue litmus paper red
  • ✔ pH range strictly less than 7

Nature of Bases

Bases are substances that yield hydroxide ($\text{OH}^-$) ions in aqueous solutions. Water-soluble bases are specifically called **Alkalis**.

Dissociation:
$\text{NaOH(aq)} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}} \text{Na}^+ + \text{OH}^-$

*All Alkalis are Bases, but not all Bases are Alkalis!

  • ✔ Bitter taste and soapy to touch
  • ✔ Turn red litmus paper blue
  • ✔ pH range strictly greater than 7

The Dilution Protocol

Diluting an acid/base decreases the concentration of $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ or $\text{OH}^-$ per unit volume. This reaction is extremely **Exothermic**.

Golden Rule: Always add Acid to Water slowly with constant stirring. NEVER add water to acid, as the sudden heat can splash acid and break glass containers.
  • ⚡ Strong Acids: Complete dissociation ($\text{HCl, H}_2\text{SO}_4$)
  • ⚡ Weak Acids: Partial dissociation ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$)

Common Representative

Pure Water, Human Blood

Clean water at neutral state has equal concentration of acid and base ions. Blood functions in a narrow window of 7.0 - 7.8.

Practical Board Board Significance

Aquatic life cannot survive if rain water pH falls below 5.6 (Acid Rain). Lime or chalk is often added to lakes to neutralize high acidity.

High-Yield Board Topics: pH in Daily Life

Tooth Decay (pH 5.5)

Bacteria degrade food debris into acid. Tooth enamel (Calcium Phosphate) corrodes below 5.5. Alkaline toothpastes are used to prevent this!

Digestive System (pH ~1.2)

HCl digests food. Overproduction causes indigestion and irritation. Magnesium Hydroxide ($\text{Mg(OH)}_2$) acts as an antacid to neutralize it.

Self-Defense (Chemical War)

Honeybee stings inject Methanoic/Formic Acid. Rubbing mild bases like baking soda neutralizes the sting and relieves pain.
